# @elena.moretti.wellness — account gone. Recovery and relaunch plan
Written 21 Sep 2026. Status check: a vidIQ public-profile lookup on `elena.moretti.wellness` returns **"Instagram profile not found."** Four days ago (audit of 17 Sep) the same lookup returned 68 followers and 8 reels. The handle no longer resolves publicly, which is what both a self-deletion and a Meta removal look like from the outside.

**First thing to establish, because everything below forks on it:** did you delete the account, or did Instagram disable it? Log in with the account's credentials. If you get "Your account has been disabled" or "We suspended your account", it was Meta. If you get "user not found" / the login simply fails with no notice, it was a self-deletion or the account is already through the deletion pipeline.

---

## 1. The 48-hour actions (do these before anything creative)

### If Instagram disabled it
1. **Appeal now, not later.** Log in with the disabled account's credentials and use the on-screen **Request a Review** prompt — that is the only appeal route Meta officially documents. The clock is roughly **30 days from disablement** before permanent deletion begins, and you generally get **one** appeal, so the wording matters. ([Meta help](https://help.instagram.com/366993040048856/), [SocialCal](https://www.socialcal.app/blog/disabled-instagram-account), [Unfollr](https://www.unfollr.com/blog/instagram-account-suspended))
2. **Do not open a replacement account while the appeal is pending.** Meta reads that as ban evasion — a separate, more serious violation that can take down the new account and prejudice the appeal on the old one. Meta associates accounts by device, IP, phone, email and payment method, so a "fresh start" from the same phone is not fresh. ([Creator Lane](https://creatorlanehq.com/blog/instagram-account-banned-2026-recovery-guide), [Inflowave](https://inflowave.io/resources/instagram-account-suspended-disabled-recovery-guide))
3. **Write the appeal to the actual likely cause** (section 2). A generic "I didn't break any rules" appeal on an account that was an undisclosed AI persona selling a supplement will fail. The appeal that has a chance says: this is an AI-generated persona account, I did not realise it required the AI-generated profile label, I will enable the label and add affiliate disclosure on reinstatement.

### If you deleted it yourself
1. Instagram keeps a self-deleted account recoverable for **30 days** — log in within that window and follow the restore prompt. After that the handle and the 68 followers are gone for good.
2. If you want the handle back and the 30 days have passed, assume it is burned and pick a new one (section 4).

### Either way, today
- **Recover the assets.** Every clip, every caption, the profile picture, the ManyChat setup if it existed. If they only lived in Instagram, rebuild the captions from `elena-page-audit-2026-09-17.md` — the hooks and CTAs are recorded there.
- **Check the Amazon Associates account is still healthy.** The affiliate link (`amzn.to/3VcALjc`, tag `nina754-20`) was promoted across eight captions with **no disclosure anywhere**. Associates ToS requires the disclosure; an account termination there costs more long-term than the Instagram page did. Add the disclosure to any other surface using that tag.
- **Stop treating the affiliate link as the business.** The page is proof that a single platform decision can zero the whole asset overnight.

## 2. Why it most likely happened, ranked

The audit already flagged every one of these as an open risk. In the four days since, the two biggest ones got much more dangerous.

**1. Undisclosed AI persona — the highest-probability cause.**
On **31 Aug 2026** Instagram renamed its "AI creator" tag to **"AI-generated profile"** and made it mandatory for accounts whose featured person is AI-generated. Enforcement started "in the coming weeks" — i.e. exactly the window this account disappeared in. The stated penalty is reach limitation (no Reels/Explore recommendation), and Instagram says it will normally notify the creator and offer the chance to self-label first. But this account was not just an unlabelled AI persona: it was an unlabelled AI persona **making health claims and selling a supplement**, which is the specific pattern the NYT investigation put on Meta's desk in July 2026 ("hundreds of AI-generated doctors, healers and wellness personalities promoting supplements"). Accounts in that pattern are being handled harder than the plain label demotion. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/31/instagram-puts-new-limits-on-undisclosed-ai-profiles/), [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2246914/instagram-will-demote-ai-generated-influencers-if-they-dont-clearly-label-their-account/), [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-updates-tags-for-ai-profiles/829235/), [iTechPost](https://www.itechpost.com/articles/237184/20260901/instagram-begins-crackdown-ai-influencers-restricts-reach-accounts.htm))

**2. The 46K ice-plunge clip.** Section 2 of the audit established that the woman in the account's only viral reel is not the woman in the other seven reels or the profile picture, and vidIQ rated that footage as real human capture. If it was sourced from somewhere else, you had a real person's footage presented as a fictional 67-year-old's face — that is misrepresentation plus a likely IP complaint, and it is the one thing on the account that could trigger removal rather than demotion. It is also the reel that pulled 91% of all reach, so it is the reel most likely to have been seen and reported. Separately, Instagram's unoriginal-content rules (extended to all surfaces on 30 Apr 2026, judged at **account level** on a rolling 30-day window) mean one reposted clip out of eight was already a meaningful share of output. ([MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/414773/instagram-algorithm-update-discourages-reposted-co.html), [ALM Corp](https://almcorp.com/blog/meta-original-content-rules-2026-facebook-instagram-creators/))

**3. Health claims without disclosure.** "Age backwards", "my blood work looks better than at 40", "here's what actually worked" attached to an NAD+ affiliate link, with zero `#ad` and zero "As an Amazon Associate…" in eight captions and no disclosure in the bio. That is an FTC problem independently of Meta, and Meta removes health-claim content on its own.

**4. New-account velocity.** Eight reels in five days on a five-day-old account with an affiliate link in the bio, from a page with no posting history, is a spam-signal profile. On its own it usually gets a shadow-limit, not a deletion; stacked on 1–3 it contributes.

**What it almost certainly was not:** the captions, the hooks, or the Mediterranean positioning. Nothing in the writing violates anything, and that material is the part of the asset worth carrying forward.

## 3. What this changes strategically

The audit's plan assumed the constraint was *reach*. The real constraint is now **survivability**, and the compliance items that were listed as housekeeping in sections 5–7 of the audit are now the load-bearing part of the model:

- An AI-persona wellness page in September 2026 is a **labelled** business or it is not a business. Instagram explicitly says the label carries **no reach penalty** when applied — the penalty is for skipping it. Toggle on, and the model is legal, demotion-free and boring. Toggle off, and you are running a page whose entire value can be deleted by an enforcement sweep you cannot appeal from a position of strength.
- **Off-platform capture is no longer optional.** 46,364 plays converted into nothing recoverable because there was no email list, no DM automation and no owned destination — when the page went, 100% of the audience went with it. The single highest-value change in the rebuild is that every reel drives to something you own.
- **Amazon Associates was already the wrong monetisation** (low single-digit percent on health/grocery; well under a dollar per bottle). Rebuilding the same funnel to the same link is rebuilding a page that needed ~50K plays to earn coffee money. Fix the offer during the rebuild, not after.

## 4. The relaunch spec (only after the appeal resolves)

Sequencing matters: if the account was disabled, **finish the appeal first**. If it is reinstated you keep the handle, the 68 followers and the 46K-play reel's ranking history. If it is denied, then relaunch — and relaunch as a compliant page, not a disguised repeat. Do not try to hide the connection to the old account; fix what got it removed instead. Meta's association signals will link the accounts regardless, and a page that is now fully labelled and disclosed has nothing to hide from.

**Profile setup (non-negotiable, day zero)**
1. **Settings → Edit profile → AI-generated profile: ON.** This is the toggle creators apply themselves; it is what keeps the account recommendation-eligible.
2. Bio, rewritten from the audit's version to be disclosure-first and still sell the follow:
   > Elena · 67 · AI-generated persona
   > Mediterranean habits my nonna kept, no filler 🫒
   > Comment OLIVE for my free 10-habit list
   > Affiliate link ⬇️
3. Every caption that names a product: `#ad · affiliate link`. Every caption, no exceptions.
4. Banned phrasing list — never write a claim about a measurable health outcome: no "age backwards", no "my blood work", no "reverses", no "cures", no "clinically". Replace with habit-and-preference language: "this is what I do", "my nonna did this", "I like it because".

**Content rules (carried over from the audit, which was right about all of them)**
5. **100% of footage generated by you.** Nothing sourced, no stock person, no UGC library, no clip you cannot regenerate on demand. The ice-plunge clip is retired permanently whatever its origin — and if it was genuinely yours, the model/prompt/seed that produced it is the default for everything.
6. **An event in the first second**, saturated colour, a person in frame, trending audio, consistent face and kitchen. No bathrobes, no landscapes, no talking heads until lip-sync passes.
7. **"I'm 67" on its own line, first, largest.** The two reels with any reach both did this.
8. **Withhold half the list** and gate the rest behind OLIVE.

**Warm-up (this is new — do not repeat the eight-in-five-days launch)**
- Days 1–3: profile complete, label on, zero posts. Follow and engage in the niche.
- Days 4–10: one reel a day, **no link in bio yet**, no keyword CTA. Pure value, build a posting history.
- Day 11: add the link and the OLIVE CTA once the account has a clean history behind it.
- Then the audit's cadence: repost the winning caption on new clips every 3–4 days, "Nonna habit N of 10" in between.

**The funnel you actually own**
9. Lead magnet PDF ("Elena's 10 Nonna Habits") behind an **email capture**, not just a DM. ManyChat OLIVE → link → email → PDF. The email list is the only part of this business Instagram cannot delete.
10. Replace or supplement the Amazon link before scaling: direct brand programs in the NAD+/longevity space (Impact, ShareASale, or the brand's own program) pay a large multiple of Amazon's health rate, which is what the competitor network in the playbook uses. Amazon Influencer storefront is the fallback.
11. **Mirror every reel to TikTok and YouTube Shorts from day one.** Same clip, same caption, three platforms. It costs one upload each and it means the next deletion costs you a third of the audience instead of all of it.

## 5. Decision checklist

| Question | Answer determines |
|---|---|
| Disabled by Meta, or self-deleted? | Appeal (30-day clock, one shot) vs. restore (30-day window) vs. rebuild |
| Was the ice-plunge clip yours? | Whether the removal is appealable in good faith, and whether that visual style is reusable |
| Is the Amazon Associates account still active? | Whether the disclosure gap has already cost you the affiliate side too |
| Do you have the clips and captions saved off-platform? | How fast the relaunch can start |

Answer the first one today — the 30-day clock runs from disablement, not from when you notice.
