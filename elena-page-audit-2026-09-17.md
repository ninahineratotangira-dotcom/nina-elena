## 2. The thing to sort out first: the winning clip isn't Elena

vidIQ's frame analysis rates the 46K reel as authentic human footage ("fluid dynamics, skin texture and water droplets follow real-world physics"). The woman in it has her hair in a bun, a thinner face, and a neon athletic top. Elena's profile picture and the other seven reels show a different woman: long wavy blonde-grey hair, linen shirts, olive groves.

Two possibilities, and the advice differs:

- **If it's a clip sourced from somewhere else** (stock, another creator, a UGC library): this is the single biggest risk on the account. Instagram's originality rules suppress accounts that repost unoriginal content, and if the original creator finds it, the reel and possibly the account go. It also explains the conversion failure: someone who liked the reel taps the profile, sees a different woman, and leaves.
- **If it's a genuinely generated clip** that happens to render more realistically: then you've accidentally found the visual bar the account needs to hit. Whatever model, prompt and seed produced it should become the default.

Either way the lesson for the visuals is the same (section 4), but please confirm which it is before building on it.

## 3. Hooks: what worked and what didn't

**Working**
- **"I'm 67" on its own line, first, largest.** The two reels with any reach both open this way. The 323-play twin of the winner buried the age at the end of line two ("…every day at 67") and died. Same caption, same bowl, same lemons.
- **A number plus a noun the viewer wants.** "these 10 skin habits", "these 10 foods". Numbered promises pull caption opens.
- **A bold, slightly outrageous claim.** "age backwards" got 3.3K on an AI clip. "my skin didn't fade" got 201.

**Not working**
- **"If you're between 40–60… before it's too late"** (420). This is an 802K hook in the wider niche, so the wording isn't the problem. The clip is (6 seconds, bathrobe, one small gesture).
- **"I tried every cream and routine"** (242). Same story. A 129K hook on a competitor account died here because the clip is static hands gliding over a hyper-smooth face and vidIQ lists five separate AI tells in it.
- **Faceless landscape + READ CAPTION** (201). The age claim only works when a face contradicts it. A cliff can't be 67.
- **Spoken hooks** (85, 45). The coffee-cortisol topic is one of the most recycled in wellness, the delivery is flat text-to-speech, there's no music, and the avatar sits frozen for 17 seconds per shot.

**Rule:** the hook wording is never the problem on this page. Every failed reel is a good hook on a slow clip.

## 4. Visuals: this is where the page is losing

Compare the two reels with the same caption:

| | 46K reel | 323 reel |
|---|---|---|
| First frame | Bright kitchen, neon pink top, big glass bowl of ice, woman about to plunge | Muted rustic kitchen, white linen, ceramic bowl, woman smiling gently |
| Action | Whole face submerged for 3s, comes up gasping, water pouring off | Lower face dipped, comes up serene, a few drops |
| Energy | High. Something is about to happen in second one | None. Nothing happens in second one |
| Realism | Real (per vidIQ) | AI: rubbery water, ripples vanish, hands frozen on the bowl, painterly skin |
| Audio | Trending lo-fi beat | Possibly posted silent |

Every one of Elena's AI clips shares the same problem: slow, serene, eyes-closed, "savouring". Bite an olive and smile. Press ice and smile. Glide oil and smile. There is no moment. The competitor AI clips that hit 50–130K all have one: doors open, a hair flip, a blown kiss, stepping out of an elevator, a son spinning his mum. The ice plunge is the same principle at full volume.

Specific fixes:
1. **Every clip needs an event in the first second.** Something moves, splashes, opens, drops, or turns. Cold water on the face is the perfect Elena-compatible event; do it properly (full plunge, gasp, dripping), not a polite dip.
2. **Colour.** The winner is the only reel with a saturated colour in it. Linen and terracotta in golden hour is beautiful and invisible in a feed.
3. **Kill the bathrobe.** Both bathrobe reels failed. It reads as a skincare ad.
4. **Kill the talking heads until the lip-sync is convincing.** Two reels, 130 plays combined, 62 seconds of the account's worst footage. If you want spoken conversion reels later, they need b-roll cutaways every 3–4 seconds so the avatar's mouth is on screen for as little time as possible.
5. **Keep a person in frame.** No landscapes.
6. **Continuity.** Elena's hair, face and kitchen should be identical across every clip. Right now there are at least two kitchens, two hair styles and (see section 2) two women.
7. **Always attach a trending track.** Check that the 12 Sep clips actually had audio; vidIQ found none on the 323 reel.

## 5. Captions: strong writing, wrong funnel

**What's good**
- The Mediterranean / nonna / olive-oil voice is a real differentiator. The six competitors are all "cellular energy" clones; nobody else in the set sounds like this. Keep it.
- Numbered lists with a one-line mechanism each ("cotton pulls at your skin while you sleep, silk doesn't") are exactly the format that gets saved.
- "Follow me first so it doesn't get buried in your requests" on the foods reel is the right gate and it produced the only keyword comments on the account.

**What's broken**
1. **The viral reel has no funnel.** 46K plays, and the caption asks for a save and a follow with no keyword, no DM, no lead magnet, no product. That's the account's entire reach so far, converted to nothing. The caption was also posted four days earlier on the 323 reel *with* a NONNA keyword CTA; the winning copy is the one where the CTA was cut.
2. **The content argues against the product.** The bio sells an NAD+ capsule. The captions say "My nonna never touched a supplement bottle", "It wasn't a supplement", "a hat does what a product can't". Only one caption in eight mentions NAD+ at all (the 420-play one). A viewer who believes Elena will not buy a supplement from her.
3. **Two keywords.** OLIVE on six reels, NONNA on one. Pick OLIVE.
4. **No affiliate disclosure anywhere.** Amazon Associates requires a clear disclosure wherever the link is promoted, and the FTC requires #ad or equivalent on affiliate captions. Zero of eight captions have it, and the bio doesn't either.
5. **The lists give everything away.** "10 habits" fully written out means there's nothing left to DM and no reason to follow. The foods reel withheld ("I'll send you my list… and their benefits") and got the comments.
6. **Are the OLIVE comments being answered?** Four people commented the keyword on 13–14 Sep. No replies are visible. If there's no DM automation (ManyChat or similar) and no manual reply within an hour, the funnel ends at the comment.

## 6. Views → followers: the fixes in order

1. **Make the profile match the clip.** Whoever appears in the reels has to be who appears in the profile picture. This is the leak that turned 46K views into a handful of follows.
2. **Rewrite the bio** so a first-time visitor knows what following gets them:
   > Elena · 67 · Mediterranean habits, no filler
   > What my nonna knew about skin, energy and ageing well 🫒
   > Comment OLIVE on any reel for my free 10-habit list
   > My one daily supplement ⬇️
3. **Pin three reels** as a "start here": the ice plunge, the foods reel, and the next strong habits reel. Nothing is pinned now.
4. **Every caption ends with the same block.** Withhold half the list, then:
   > Comment OLIVE and I'll DM you the full list with what I use for each. Follow first or Instagram won't let me reply 🫒
5. **Series, not standalones.** "Nonna habit 1 of 10" gives a reason to come back. The playbook has this structure; run it with the Mediterranean topic list (olive oil on skin, no soap, sea salt soak, silk pillowcase, hat before sunscreen, oil on hands, walk after meals, dinner before dark, warm water before coffee, anchovies).
6. **Repost the winning caption with new clips every 3–4 days.** Same words, new event (plunge, splash, cold cloth snap). The 46K/323 pair proves the clip is the variable.

## 7. Followers → money: the fixes in order

1. **Reconcile the product with the story.** Two honest options:
   - **Keep NAD+ and make it Elena's one exception.** "My nonna had the olive oil, the walk and the sun. She didn't have this. It's the one thing I take that she couldn't." Put it as item 1 or 2 in every list, every time, with the same line: "the exact one I take is in my bio". Never again write "it wasn't a supplement".
   - **Or switch the product to match the content.** A premium olive oil, a silk pillowcase, or a sea-salt soak all sit naturally in the captions and cost nothing to explain. Lower price, lower commission, but zero friction with the persona.
2. **Check the Amazon commission before scaling.** Amazon Associates pays low single-digit percent on health and grocery. At the Thinbi price point that's well under a dollar per bottle. The competitor network uses direct brand programs (lvnta, linktw.in cloaked links) paying a large multiple of that. Search the NAD+ brands for an affiliate program on Impact, ShareASale or their own site, or apply to Amazon Influencer for a storefront, before sending real traffic.
3. **Build the DM funnel.** One-page PDF ("Elena's 10 Nonna Habits"), ManyChat keyword OLIVE, follow gate, product link as habit 1, one follow-up message 24 hours later. Until this exists, every OLIVE comment is a lost lead.
4. **Disclose.** Add "#ad · affiliate link" to every caption that mentions the product, and "As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases" or "affiliate link" to the bio while the link is Amazon.
5. **One conversion reel a week**, only once the avatar's lip-sync passes. Until then, the caption is the conversion surface.
