# Compliance — AU/NZ

Not legal advice. This is the operator's checklist of what regulators in these two countries actually act on. Get an accountant before you cross the GST threshold.

---

## 1. You are the seller. Not the supplier.

Under the **Australian Consumer Law**, a dropshipper is the **seller of record** — the ACCC holds *you* responsible, not the warehouse in Melbourne or the factory in Shenzhen. New Zealand's **Consumer Guarantees Act** works the same way. "My supplier sent the wrong thing" is not a defence available to you.

Source: [Sprintlaw — dropshipping legal guide AU](https://sprintlaw.com.au/articles/dropshipping-decoded-legal-guide-australian-ecommerce-entrepreneurs/)

---

## 2. The three things they actually fine people for

### a) "No refunds" policies
The ACCC swept **2,000+ Australian online retailers** looking for misleading returns and T&Cs. Businesses displaying "no refunds" without proper qualification are "regularly investigated and penalised." You cannot contract out of consumer guarantees in either country.

**What to write instead:** state that you refund/replace/repair for faulty, not-as-described or unfit-for-purpose goods as required by the ACL/CGA, *and separately* state your (optional, your choice) change-of-mind policy.

### b) Delivery times you don't meet
Any delivery timeframe on a product page is a **representation** subject to misleading-conduct rules. "Estimated delivery 3–5 business days" that actually takes 2–3 weeks is misleading conduct. Businesses must not accept payment when they know or should know they can't supply by the promised date.

**This is the single biggest legal risk in dropshipping**, and it's the reason `02-suppliers.md` insists on local warehouses. State the *real* range from your sample test, then add a buffer.

### c) Product claims
Especially health, therapeutic, safety or performance claims. If the product touches health in any way, AU's TGA and NZ's Medsafe rules can apply on top of consumer law. Don't repeat a supplier's marketing copy — suppliers routinely write claims that are illegal to publish in AU/NZ.

Sources: [ACCC — consumer rights and guarantees](https://www.accc.gov.au/consumers/buying-products-and-services/consumer-rights-and-guarantees) · [ACCC — supplying products paid for](https://www.accc.gov.au/business/selling-products-and-services/supplying-products-or-services-that-are-paid-for) · [ACCC sweep media release](https://www.accc.gov.au/media-release/accc-sweep-uncovers-concerning-online-shopping-return-policies-and-terms-and-conditions) · [Bird & Bird analysis](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2025/australia/acccs-sweeping-investigation-exposes-dubious-e-commerce-returns)

## 3. Tax

| | NZ | AU |
|---|---|---|
| Register for GST when | Turnover exceeds **or is likely to exceed** NZ$60,000 in any 12 months | A$75,000 |
| Rate | 15% | 10% |
| Selling into the other country | AU GST may apply to your sales into AU once you pass A$75k of AU sales | NZ GST may apply on sales into NZ once past NZ$60k |

**Practical sequencing:** launch as a sole trader below the threshold. The moment monthly revenue × 12 approaches NZ$60k, register — the "likely to exceed" wording means you register on the trajectory, not the history. Price GST-inclusive from day one so registering doesn't force a price rise that kills conversion.

Source: [IRD GST registration](https://www.ird.govt.nz/gst/gst-for-overseas-businesses/gst-on-low-value-imported-goods/registering-for-gst-on-low-value-imported-goods)

## 4. Store pages you must have before your first ad

- [ ] **Shipping policy** — real dispatch time, real delivery range, per region, from your sample test
- [ ] **Returns & refunds** — consumer guarantees honoured + your change-of-mind terms stated separately
- [ ] **Terms of service**
- [ ] **Privacy policy** — you're collecting emails and running Meta pixel; NZ Privacy Act 2020 applies
- [ ] **Contact page with a real, monitored email address** and business name
- [ ] **About page** — a real one. This is also your highest-converting trust asset.

Shopify auto-generates serviceable drafts for the policy pages under Settings → Policies. Use them as a base, then edit the shipping and refund sections to match reality. Don't ship the defaults unedited — they contain placeholders.

---

## 5. Advertising platform policy (separate from the law, and enforced faster)

Meta and TikTok will disable an account for claims the law would only warn you about. Before choosing a product, check it isn't in a restricted category: health/medical devices, weight loss, supplements with claims, before/after body imagery, anything implying a personal attribute.

A disabled ad account at week 3 of a launch is a worse outcome than a regulator letter at month 12. Check first.
