# Compliance: What You Can and Cannot Say (NZ + AU)

> Not legal advice. This is a practical working guide built from the regulators' own published definitions. If you scale past hobby revenue, pay a lawyer for an hour.

## The one rule that matters

**A product becomes a regulated therapeutic good because of the CLAIM you make about it, not because of what it is.**

The TGA states plainly that it "does not assess cosmetics unless they make therapeutic claims." A silk eye mask is a textile. The same eye mask advertised as *"treats insomnia"* is an unregistered therapeutic good, and you are now in breach.

This is entirely within your control. The copy in `content/` is written to stay on the safe side of this line.

## Who regulates you

| Jurisdiction | Body | Governs |
|---|---|---|
| Australia | **TGA** (Therapeutic Goods Act 1989) | Therapeutic claims, ARTG registration |
| Australia | **ACCC** (Australian Consumer Law) | Misleading/deceptive conduct, refunds |
| New Zealand | **Medsafe** (Medicines Act) | Therapeutic claims |
| New Zealand | **Commerce Commission** (Fair Trading Act 1986) | Misleading claims |
| New Zealand | **Consumer Guarantees Act 1993** | Mandatory refund/repair rights |
| Both | **ASA / AANA** | Advertising standards |

The TGA's definition of a therapeutic good — anything used for *"preventing, diagnosing, curing or alleviating a disease, ailment, defect or injury"* or *"influencing, inhibiting or modifying a physiological process."* Read that second clause twice. It is much broader than most sellers assume.

## Banned phrases — never use these

These convert your product into a therapeutic good:

- ❌ treats / cures / heals / remedies
- ❌ insomnia, sleep apnoea, apnea, sleep disorder, restless leg syndrome
- ❌ clinically proven, medically proven, doctor recommended *(unless you hold the evidence and the endorsement)*
- ❌ reduces cortisol / lowers blood pressure / regulates melatonin / slows heart rate
- ❌ therapeutic, medical grade, pharmaceutical grade
- ❌ anxiety, depression, ADHD, or any named condition
- ❌ alleviates, relieves, prevents *(when attached to a condition)*
- ❌ "increases deep sleep by X%" — any physiological quantification
- ❌ before/after health outcome comparisons

## Safe phrasing — use these instead

Describe **the object, the sensation, and the ritual.** Never the physiological outcome.

| Instead of ❌ | Write ✅ |
|---|---|
| "Treats insomnia" | "Made for people who struggle to switch off" |
| "Blocks light to boost melatonin" | "Blocks 100% of light" *(a measurable property of the object)* |
| "Clinically proven to deepen sleep" | "Designed around how a wind-down routine actually works" |
| "Reduces anxiety" | "A weighted, grounding feel across the eyes" |
| "Therapeutic weighted pillow" | "Gently weighted, 280g" |
| "Cures restless nights" | "Built for a calmer end to the day" |

**The test:** does the sentence describe a *property of the object* or a *change in the customer's body*? Properties are safe. Bodily changes are claims.

### Naming the brand's guide
The digital product is titled **The Slow Wave Protocol**. "Slow-wave sleep" is a descriptive sleep-stage term, which is fine as a brand reference — but the guide must never claim to *increase* slow-wave sleep. It is framed throughout as **environment and routine education**, not treatment.

## Mandatory disclaimer

Appears on the digital product page, in the guide's first page, and in the site footer:

> This guide is general educational information about sleep environment and routine. It is not medical advice, and it is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any condition. If you have ongoing sleep difficulties, please speak to your doctor.

## Consumer law obligations (these are not optional)

**New Zealand — Consumer Guarantees Act:** goods must be of acceptable quality and fit for purpose. You cannot contract out of this for consumer sales. A "no refunds" policy is itself a Fair Trading Act breach.

**Australia — Australian Consumer Law:** consumers have guaranteed rights to repair, replacement or refund on major failures, regardless of your stated policy.

**Digital goods:** the refund policy in `content/` offers refunds on the digital product only where it has not been downloaded — this is a legitimate and common position, and it is stated clearly up front rather than buried.

## Pre-publish checklist

Before any product page, ad, or social post goes live:

- [ ] No banned phrase from the list above
- [ ] Every claim describes the object, not the body
- [ ] No named medical condition anywhere
- [ ] No implied clinical evidence
- [ ] Disclaimer present on digital product
- [ ] Shipping times stated honestly (overstating delivery speed is a Fair Trading Act breach)
- [ ] Any "was/now" pricing reflects a genuine former price — fake RRP anchoring is specifically enforced by both the ACCC and NZ Commerce Commission
