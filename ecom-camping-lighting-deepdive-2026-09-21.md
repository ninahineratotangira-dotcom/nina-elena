# Deep dive: camping lighting — and why it fails
**21 September 2026**

Verdict up front: **camping lighting is dead as a dropshipping product in AU/NZ.** Bunnings sells the exact viral product for $9.98. The detail is below, along with what the research turned up instead — which is a materially better business.

---

## 1. The kill shot

The hero product from the viral videos — a retractable string light that pulls 10–15m of lights out of a palm-sized disc — is already a stocked line item at Bunnings:

| Product | Retailer | Price |
|---|---|---|
| **Click 10m Rechargeable LED Camping Retractable String Lighting** | **Bunnings AU and Bunnings NZ** | **$9.98** |
| Click Solar Lantern + Retractable 12m String Light with Power Bank | Bunnings AU and NZ | stocked |
| Solar String Lights | Kmart AU | $16 |
| Lantern with Speaker | Kmart AU | $10 (was $20) |
| Full LED/rechargeable camping lantern range | BCF, Anaconda | stocked |

Bunnings carries it **in both countries**, under their own Click house brand, at $9.98. Kmart undercuts on the solar variant at $16 with free delivery over $65.

There is no margin structure in which you win this. A landed unit from China at $6–10, plus the new NZ border levy, plus freight, plus Shopify and payment fees, plus a customer acquisition cost of $15–40 in a contested category, against a $9.98 shelf price two minutes from your customer's house. It is not close.

**I recommended this lane last turn on the strength of the viral video data. Checking local retail shelves was the step that was missing, and it reverses the recommendation.** The videos were real — but most of them were pointed at US and global audiences, where this product isn't a hardware-store staple. In your market it is.

### A second red flag in the same category

Three separate TikTok accounts are running the *identical* caption — "Solar auto-charging lamp, zero electricity cost forever" — with the same hashtags and the same #tiktokshop tag:

- @chinesecabbage26 — 1.3M views (166× median)
- @themiles4 — 1.3M views (79× median)
- @alicefaye32 — 423K views (77× median)

That's not organic demand, that's a saturated affiliate campaign with an army already running it. When you see one caption across multiple unrelated accounts, the product is at the end of its cycle, not the start.

## 2. The rule this gives you, which is worth more than the product

**Viral video reach is not market opportunity. Check the shelf before you check the trend.**

Before committing to any product, in this order:

1. Search it on **bunnings.com.au**, **kmart.com.au**, **anaconda.com.au**, **bcf.com.au**, **amazon.com.au**, **Temu**.
2. If it's stocked at any of them under ~$30 — **dead, walk away.**
3. Search the exact caption of the viral video in quotes. If more than two unrelated accounts are running it, you're late.
4. Only then look at suppliers.

Applied honestly, this rule kills most "winning products" lists. That's the point — it kills them before they cost you money instead of after.

### The single best illustration of the thesis

**@accelerateoffgridtouring**, 255K views at **213× their median**. The hook: *"Just have gotten into this 300 and they have drilled straight into their dash."* The product is a bracket that mounts a UHF radio in a specific Toyota model without drilling into the dashboard.

Look at why that product is defensible in a way a string light never was:

- **Vehicle-specific.** It fits one model. That means SKU sprawl, which is exactly why Bunnings and Kmart won't touch it — big-box retail needs one SKU that sells to everyone.
- **Solves an expensive, emotional problem.** Nobody wants to drill a hole in a $100,000 vehicle.
- **The buyer isn't price-shopping.** Someone who spent six figures on a 300 Series is not agonising over $120.
- **Instant demo.** Problem on screen in two seconds (a drilled dash), solution in the next five.
- **You can't Temu it.** The fitment is the product.

@maxvoltagestore is the same shape: an adapter that lets Makita, Ozito and Ryobi batteries — which every Australian tradie already owns a pile of — run a camping fridge. Clever, specific, unstockable by a general retailer.

## 4. Product archetypes worth sourcing in this lane

Ranked by fit for a from-zero operator:

**1. No-drill / vehicle-specific mounting hardware** — UHF brackets, device mounts, accessory rails. Evidence: 213× outlier. Price band roughly $80–200. Highest defensibility because fitment is the moat. Biggest risk: you must get compatibility exactly right, and you need to carry several vehicle variants.

**2. 12V power accessories and adapters** — battery adapters, DC distribution, fridge power solutions. Evidence: 48× outlier, and there's a genuine boom in portable power. Price band $60–250. Risk: electrical goods carry compliance obligations in AU/NZ (check ACMA/EESS requirements before importing — this is non-negotiable and can sink you).

**3. Vehicle-mounted lighting — *not* string lights** — magnetic spotlights, remote-controlled work lights, bar lighting. Evidence: 238× on a 96-follower account. This is the one place lighting survives, because it's above the commodity band and not in Bunnings' camping aisle at $9.98.

**4. Storage and quick-deploy systems** — drawer inserts, tailgate tables, awning accessories. Evidence: 7.2M views, 75×. The unfold *is* the ad. Higher freight cost, but the demo sells itself.

**Deliberately not recommended:** hardshell rooftop tents. The market data flags $1,500–2,500 as the highest-demand bracket, which is true — and it's also $1,500–2,500 of your capital per unit, oversized freight, and a product where a fitment or quality failure is catastrophic. Wrong first product, right market.

## 5. The honest catch on timing

This lane is a better business than camping lighting. It is probably **not** a Christmas business for you.

Vehicle-specific products need fitment verification, which means samples, which means test-fitting on actual vehicles. From a standing start on 21 September, doing that properly and having stock in an AU warehouse before Black Friday is tight to the point of reckless.

Two honest options:

**A. Take the slower, better path.** Target a **January–February launch** into the AU touring season, which runs well past summer. Use October–December to source properly, verify fitment, build credibility in the community, and bank creative. You'd miss Christmas and gain a real business.

**B. Run a simple Christmas product *and* build the 4x4 lane behind it.** Pick something low-fitment-risk in archetype 3 or 4 for December cash flow and learning, while sourcing the vehicle-specific range for Q1.

I'd take A unless you need revenue before Christmas specifically. The 4x4 buyer isn't seasonal in the way a beach product is — touring runs year-round in Australia, so you're not missing a one-shot window.

**The credibility caveat, stated plainly:** this community can smell an outsider instantly. @4wd247, @aussiearvos and @outbackoffroadnt are trusted because they clearly live it. If you don't know 4x4s, you'll need a genuine partner or advisor who does, or the content won't land regardless of how good the product is. That's a real barrier and you should weigh it before committing.

## 6. Next steps, if you take this lane

1. **Pick one vehicle platform to start.** Toyota HiLux, Ford Ranger, LandCruiser 300, or Prado — the highest-volume touring platforms in Australia. One platform, done properly, beats five done vaguely.
2. **Source three suppliers with AU warehousing.** Dropshipzone and Ozdingo dispatch same-day from Australian warehouses; CJDropshipping holds AU stock on some lines. For vehicle-specific parts you'll more likely need direct manufacturer contact.
3. **Check compliance before you order anything electrical.** ACMA and EESS requirements for 12V electrical goods in Australia. Get this wrong and the stock is unsellable.
4. **Run the margin maths at $80–250 retail**, not $10–30. Landed cost + levy + GST + freight + fees + CAC. Target 3× landed cost minimum.
5. **Find your credibility source** — a partner, an advisor, or a creator to work with. This is a prerequisite, not a nice-to-have.
6. **Re-screen every candidate against section 2** before spending a dollar.
